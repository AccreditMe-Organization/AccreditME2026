Arabic is 16px over — resolved for both languages
Name and Email share one row, 50/50, in both languages. Five blocks become four rows: English 12 + 4 × 71 + 3 × 8 + 12 = 332, Arabic 12 + 4 × 76 + 24 + 12 = 352. Both fit with room for the conditional sentences below. The single-column A drawing shows the fields and their rules. The build uses the paired row, as the Arabic drawing does.

Org unit, when the tenant has no active units

The field is replaced by a sentence (artboard 10's zero-field rule), not hidden and not disabled. This is the first tenant admin's case. They're usually inviting colleagues before building the structure.

The shadow's sign flips. This is a deliberate instruction, not something that mirrors by itself.
box-shadow has no logical form, so its x-offset is physical. English: 6px 0 8px -6px. Arabic: -6px 0 8px -6px. Set it from :dir(rtl) or a --shadow-x custom property that the dir flips. One place where I've drawn differently from the brief: the shadow falls on the column's inner edge, the one facing the data, which is left of the column in Arabic. That's the edge the scrolling cells pass beneath, and the shadow exists to show that. On the outer edge it would sit against the table's own border, be clipped by overflow, and show nothing. If “toward the outer edge” meant something else, say so and I'll redraw it.
