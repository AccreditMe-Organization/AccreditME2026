Measured, compact density (5 field blocks, so the rule applies): block = label 18 + 2 + control 32 + 2 + slot 17 = 71. Body 12 + 5 × 71 + 4 × 8 + 12 = 411 / 420. Arabic: block 20 + 2 + 32 + 2 + 20 = 76, body 12 + 5 × 76 + 32 + 12 = 436, which is 16 over. That's resolved below, the same way in both languages.
Arabic is 16px over — resolved for both languages
Name and Email share one row, 50/50, in both languages. Five blocks become four rows: English 12 + 4 × 71 + 3 × 8 + 12 = 332, Arabic 12 + 4 × 76 + 24 + 12 = 352. Both fit with room for the conditional sentences below. The single-column A drawing shows the fields and their rules. The build uses the paired row, as the Arabic drawing does.
The two conditional rules, drawn as the field's replacement
Org unit, when the tenant has no active units
ⓘYour organisation has no units yet, so this person won't be placed in one. Set up units
The field is replaced by a sentence (artboard 10's zero-field rule), not hidden and not disabled. This is the first tenant admin's case. They're usually inviting colleagues before building the structure.
Manager, when inviting the root unit's own head
ⓘNot needed. As head of Al Nakheel Specialist Hospital, they report to no one.
This appears as soon as Position is a head-conferring position and Org unit is the root. Change either and the Manager field comes back, required, with any earlier choice restored (artboard 10: switching is non-destructive). The rule reads the same inputs the service checks, so the dialog never makes a claim the server would refuse.
